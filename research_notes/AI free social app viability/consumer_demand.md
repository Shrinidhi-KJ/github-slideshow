# Consumer Demand and Sentiment: AI-Generated Content on Social Media (for an AI-free Instagram-like app)

Research date: 2026-11-02. Every figure is dated. Where I could only reach a secondary write-up, or a source is weak, I say so.

## 1. What do recent surveys say about how users feel about AI-generated content / "AI slop"?

### Takeaway
Across many 2024-2026 surveys, the public is consistently and increasingly uneasy about AI-generated content. Majorities say it matters to know what is real (76% Pew US; ~90% Getty global), and only about 1 in 8 are comfortable with fully AI-made media (Reuters Institute). But the demand people actually voice is mostly for **labels and transparency**. The share who want AI content **banned outright** is far smaller: about 21% of US social media users in CNET's survey. A strict "zero-AI" stance has a real but minority base. "Know what is real" is the mass-market position.

### Cited Findings
**Pew Research Center (US). Published 2025-09-17. Fielded June 9-15, 2025. n=5,023 US adults.**
- 76% say it is extremely or very important to be able to tell whether pictures, videos and text were made by AI or by people. 53% are not too or not at all confident they can do so. — [Pew, Sep 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/science/2025/09/17/how-americans-view-ai-and-its-impact-on-people-and-society/)
- 50% are more concerned than excited about the increased use of AI in daily life, up from 37% in 2021. 10% are more excited than concerned and 38% equally both. — [Pew, Sep 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/science/2025/09/17/how-americans-view-ai-and-its-impact-on-people-and-society/)
- 53% say AI will worsen people's ability to think creatively (16% improve). Among adults under 30, 61% say it will make people worse at creative thinking and 58% say worse at forming meaningful relationships. — [Pew, Sep 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/science/2025/09/17/how-americans-view-ai-and-its-impact-on-people-and-society/)

**CNET survey (US social media users). n≈2,400. Published roughly Feb 2026, syndicated May 2026. Exact fielding dates and pollster not confirmed; the CNET page could not be fetched.**
- 94% of US adults who use social media believe they encounter AI-generated content while scrolling. Only 44% say they are confident they can tell real photos and videos from AI-generated ones. — [CNET via search excerpt](https://www.cnet.com/tech/services-and-software/ai-slop-is-destroying-the-internet-these-are-the-people-fighting-to-save-it/); [CNET survey page](https://www.cnet.com/tech/services-and-software/cnet-survey-ai-slop-confidence-in-spotting-ai-waning/)
- 51% say AI-generated content should be better labeled. **21% support a total ban** on AI content on social media. Sample is 2,400. — [WGAL/Hearst, 2026-05-14](https://www.wgal.com/article/survey-shows-growing-frustration-ai-content-social-media/71311724)

**Reuters Institute, Generative AI and News Report 2026 (YouGov fieldwork). About 19,000 respondents (n=18,921) in 9 countries.**
- Only 12% are comfortable with news made entirely by AI, rising to 19% with human oversight, versus 67% for human-made news. The UK shows one of the widest gaps: 5% comfortable with fully AI news versus 75% for fully human. — [What's New in Publishing summary](https://whatsnewinpublishing.substack.com/p/ai-use-is-soaring-but-audiences-dont); [Reuters Institute 2026](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/generative-ai-and-news-report-2026-standalone-tools-information-infrastructure)
- In no country are more than 1 in 6 comfortable with news made entirely by AI. 22% of 18-24s are comfortable, versus 7% of those 55+. — [Reuters Institute 2026](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/generative-ai-and-news-report-2026-standalone-tools-information-infrastructure)
- The 2025 edition similarly found only 12% comfortable with fully AI-generated news (a "comfort gap"). — [Reuters Institute 2025](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/generative-ai-and-news-report-2025-how-people-think-about-ais-role-journalism-and-society)

**Getty Images VisualGPS, "Building Trust in the Age of AI". Published 2024-04-30. More than 30,000 adults in 25 countries, 2022-2024 (incl. US, UK).**
- Almost 90% of consumers globally want to know whether an image was created using AI. — [Getty newsroom, Apr 2024](https://newsroom.gettyimages.com/en/getty-images/nearly-90-of-consumers-want-transparency-on-ai-images-finds-getty-images-report)
- 98% agree "authentic" images and videos are pivotal in establishing trust. 87% say it is important for an image to be authentic. 76% agree "It's getting to the point where I can't tell if an image is real." — [Getty newsroom](https://newsroom.gettyimages.com/en/getty-images/nearly-90-of-consumers-want-transparency-on-ai-images-finds-getty-images-report)
- 78% agree an AI-generated image cannot be considered "authentic." 66% prefer brands that use real, unedited photos in ads. Consumers are more than twice as likely to prefer "real" images (38%) as AI images (15%) in marketing. 74% agree an AI image can be just as realistic as a photo. — [CX Network interview with Getty](https://www.cxnetwork.com/artificial-intelligence/interviews/ai-generated-images-customer-trust-advertising)
- Concern about how AI images and videos are made rose from 57% to 63% year over year. UK figures are slightly above the global average. — [Getty VisualGPS PDF](http://reports.gettyimages.com/VisualGPS-Building-Trust-In-AI.pdf)

**Other consumer and marketing surveys (2024-2025). Mostly reached via secondary write-ups, so lower confidence.**
- Baringa, fielded January 2025 in five countries: 70% would be uncomfortable consuming fully AI-generated creative content, and 54% uncomfortable with AI-assisted content. — [Variety VIP](https://variety.com/vip/most-consumers-wary-of-fully-ai-generated-creative-content-survey-1236347518/)
- Clutch: 48% feel negatively about brands that use AI to create social media content. Undated, about 2025. — [Clutch](https://clutch.co/resources/ai-welcomed-social-media)
- Retail Technology Show: 53% of shoppers mistrust AI-generated social content, rising to 58% among Gen Z. About 2025. — [Mission Media](https://missionmedia.asia/shoppers-mistrust-ai-generated-social-content-survey/)
- Billion Dollar Boy, June-July 2025, US/UK: 32% say AI is negatively disrupting the creator economy, up from 18% in 2023. — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/consumers-rejecting-ai-generated-creator-content)
- CivicScience, July 2025: 31% say AI in ads makes them less likely to choose a brand. — [search summary of eMarketer/CivicScience coverage](https://www.emarketer.com/content/consumers-rejecting-ai-generated-creator-content)
- YouGov 2024 (US): about half of consumers say AI-generated ads are a turnoff. — [CX Network citing Forbes/YouGov, Nov 2024](https://www.cxnetwork.com/artificial-intelligence/interviews/ai-generated-images-customer-trust-advertising)
- YouGov 2026 (US): 39% of Americans say AI's impact on society is mostly or very negative. A separate summary reports the share saying AI causes more harm than benefit rose from 31% to 39% in one year. — [YouGov](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/55709-americans-are-worried-about-using-ai-but-that-isnt-stopping-them-from-using-it); [NBC15 summary](https://www.facebook.com/mynbc15/posts/what-is-ai-slop-and-how-is-it-impacting-americans/1447217107442577/)
- Economist/YouGov, September 18-21, 2026: concern about AI harms is "rising quickly," though many also expect benefits. — [YouGov, Sep 2026](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/55608-many-americans-expect-big-benefits-from-ai-artificial-intelligence-concerns-are-rising-quickly-september-18-21-2026-economist-yougov-poll)
- OpenSurvey (Korea-based, n=900 users of YouTube/Instagram/TikTok): 49.9% find AI-generated content artificial or awkward. Weak source and date unclear. — [World Today News](https://www.world-today-news.com/half-of-social-media-users-find-ai-content-artificial/)

### Inferences
- The modal consumer wants **transparency and control**: labels, filters, "know what's real." A smaller but substantial minority, about 1 in 5 in the US, wants AI content gone entirely. That 21% "ban" figure is probably the best proxy for the core addressable segment of a strict AI-free app, though stated support does not equal switching.
- Discomfort is highest for **fully** AI-generated content about **people** and for creative work. Getty found AI depictions of non-human subjects were seen as less misleading. An AI-free app's value proposition is strongest around real people, real places and real art.
- The direction of travel is negative: Pew concern rose from 37% to 50% (2021 to 2025), Getty concern from 57% to 63%, and Billion Dollar Boy creator-economy negativity from 18% to 32%.

### Gaps
- I found no Ipsos, Morning Consult or Adobe figures specifically on AI content in social feeds within the call budget. Adobe's Content Authenticity surveys likely exist but were not retrieved.
- I could not access the CNET survey page directly. Pollster, fielding dates and generational breakdowns are unconfirmed.
- I found no platform-specific attitude splits (Instagram vs TikTok vs Pinterest) from a rigorous pollster.

## 2. What backlash events have occurred?

### Takeaway
2025-2026 brought a string of high-profile rejections of AI-native social products, plus defensive policy moves by incumbents. The clearest market signal is that **OpenAI shut down the Sora social app in March 2026**, about six months after launch. Meta's AI personas were pulled within days in January 2025, and Vibes was mocked at launch. Pinterest, YouTube and Instagram have each moved to label, filter or demonetize AI content. These are concessions that partly undercut an AI-free entrant's differentiation.

### Cited Findings
**Meta AI character accounts (January 2025)**
- Meta removed its AI character accounts on Instagram and Facebook after users criticized them as "creepy and unnecessary." About a dozen such AI profiles had existed since late 2023. — [NBC News, Jan 2025](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/social-media/meta-ai-insta-shuts-character-instagram-fb-accounts-user-outcry-rcna186177)
- Users surfaced problematic personas such as "Liv," described as a "Proud Black queer momma." Meta attributed the removal to a bug that prevented users from blocking the accounts. — [Mashable](https://mashable.com/article/meta-deletes-ai-character-profiles-after-backlash); [Facebook group repost of Meta statement](https://www.facebook.com/groups/1404116417142065/posts/1639981500222221/)

**Meta "Vibes" AI video feed (launched 2025-09-25)**
- Top replies to Zuckerberg's launch post included "gang nobody wants this" and "Bro's posting ai slop on his own app." — [TechCrunch, 2025-09-25](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/25/meta-launches-vibes-a-short-form-video-feed-of-ai-slop/)
- Despite the mockery, Vibes reached about 2 million DAU as of 2025-11-09, growing about 1% week over week (Meta internal data seen by Business Insider). India had 704,000 DAU (+22% WoW), Brazil 114,000, and Europe 23,000 after its 2025-11-06 launch. About 52% of returning users prompted the AI versus about 30% who only scrolled. — [Business Insider, Nov 2025](https://www.businessinsider.com/meta-vibes-ai-internal-documents-show-daily-active-users-2025-11)
- For context, Meta's "family daily active people" was 3.54B in September 2025, so 2M Vibes DAU is under 0.1% of the Meta user base. — [Business Insider, Nov 2025](https://www.businessinsider.com/meta-vibes-ai-internal-documents-show-daily-active-users-2025-11)

**OpenAI Sora social app (launched about Sep/Oct 2025; shutdown announced 2026-03-24)**
- Appfigures data: downloads fell 32% month over month in December 2025. There were 9.6M lifetime downloads and $1.4M consumer spend as of late January 2026. Many users did not want others using their likeness. — [TechCrunch, 2026-01-29](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/29/openais-sora-app-is-struggling-after-its-stellar-launch/)
- Retention reportedly collapsed: Day 1 10%, Day 7 2%, Day 30 1%, approaching 0% by Day 60. The data is attributed to a16z's Olivia Moore and arrives secondhand via Medium. — [Medium summary](https://medium.com/@cherc7888/sora-app-from-viral-sensation-to-1-30-day-retention-ef939c086136)
- OpenAI announced on 2026-03-24 that it was shuttering the Sora app, framed around cost-cutting. — [CNBC, 2026-03-24](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/24/openai-shutters-short-form-video-app-sora-as-company-reels-in-costs.html); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/openai-shuttering-sora-video-generating-service-rcna264989)
- The Sora API was discontinued on 2026-09-24. Users reported the app closing on 2026-04-26 (Reddit; unverified). — [OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001152-what-to-know-about-the-sora-discontinuation); [Reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/SoraAi/comments/1swtuyb/i_thought_sora_was_going_to_shut_down_on_april/)
- One blog claims the app was losing about $1M per day and that a $1B Disney deal was pulled. This is unverified and from a low-reliability source. — [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/sora-1m-per-day-loss-disney-1b-ai-video-economics)

**Pinterest AI slop complaints and controls (2025)**
- After months of Reddit complaints that AI slop was degrading Pinterest's core use (finding real-world ideas), Pinterest added "AI modified" labels in April 2025. — [TechCrunch, 2025-04-30](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/30/pinterest-launches-new-tools-to-fight-ai-slop/)
- Pinterest later let users dial down GenAI content in AI-prone categories (beauty, art, fashion, home decor) via "refine your recommendations." AI content cannot be fully switched off. Rollout was web/Android first, then iOS (late 2025; exact date unconfirmed). — [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/ai/tired-of-ai-slop-pinterest-lets-you-take-back-control-of-your-feed); [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/pinterest-ups-genai-controls-address-user--advertiser-concerns); [NBC](https://www.nbcmiami.com/news/national-international/ai-slop-platforms-dial-it-down/3756621/)

**YouTube monetization policy (effective 2025-07-15)**
- YouTube renamed its "repetitious content" policy to "inauthentic content," calling the change "minor." It clarifies that mass-produced or repetitive content is ineligible for monetization, explicitly including "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates… without adding the creator's original, authentic insights." — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en-GB); [Exemplary AI explainer](https://exemplary.ai/blog/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy)

**Instagram / Adam Mosseri year-end essay (posted on Threads about 2025-12-31)**
- "Looking forward to 2026, one new significant shift is that authenticity is becoming infinitely reproducible." Also: "it will be more practical to fingerprint real media than fake media." — [Tubefilter, 2026-01-06](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/01/06/adam-mosseri-instagram-warning-generative-ai-deepfakes/); [Threads post](https://www.threads.com/@mosseri/post/DS76UiklIDf)
- Secondary coverage quotes him saying "Authenticity is fast becoming a scarce resource" and predicting more content will be AI-edited than fully AI-created. It also reports he called for camera makers to cryptographically sign real media. Critics read this as Instagram conceding it cannot contain slop. — [Business Today, 2026-01-02](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/technology/news/story/instagram-chief-adam-mosseri-warns-online-authenticity-is-at-risk-amid-ai-boom-509084-2026-01-02); [Implicator.ai](https://www.implicator.ai/instagrams-head-just-admitted-defeat-on-ai-slop-and-wants-camera-makers-to-fix-it/); [Om Malik](https://om.co/2026/01/07/who-decides-whats-real-in-the-age-of-ai-instagram-does/)

**Artist exodus to Cara (June 2024)**
- After Meta announced it would train AI on user content from 2024-06-26, the AI-free artist network Cara grew from about 40,000 to 650,000 users in one week. It reached the Top 5 of the US App Store. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cara_(app)); [Colossal, 2024-06-03](https://www.thisiscolossal.com/2024/06/cara-app/)
- Cara later reported passing 1M users, 4M posts and 5M images (blog post, date not confirmed). Its App Store pitch reads: "Tired of AI-generated content? Our AI detector automatically filters out AI images." — [Cara blog](https://blog.cara.app/blog/finances-and-future-of-cara); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/cara-art-social/id6450934458)

### Inferences
- Pure-AI social feeds have so far failed as standalone consumer products. Sora shut down in about 6 months, and Vibes is tiny relative to Meta. That suggests people do not want AI-*only* social media. It does not prove they will switch to AI-*free* social media.
- Cara is the closest precedent for an AI-free visual network. It spiked on a trigger event (Meta's AI-training policy) to about 1M users, an order of magnitude below mass-market social. Trigger events can drive bursts of adoption, but a niche ceiling is a real risk.
- Incumbents are co-opting the "authenticity" message: Pinterest filters, YouTube demonetization, and Mosseri's "fingerprint real media." An AI-free entrant must offer something stronger than labels, such as verification or provenance, to differentiate.

### Gaps
- No public long-run retention or MAU data for Cara, or for other "no-AI" apps after 2024.
- The Vibes usage trajectory after November 2025 was not found.
- I could not confirm Sora app final user numbers or Disney deal details from primary sources.

## 3. Is there a stated vs revealed preference gap?

### Takeaway
Yes, strongly. People say they dislike AI content, yet it is algorithmically abundant and highly engaged with. More than 20% of YouTube Shorts and about 59% of TikToks shown to new accounts are AI slop. Slop channels have earned 63B+ views and an estimated ~$117M a year. Much of the engagement comes from users who do not realize the content is synthetic, since only 44% feel confident they can tell. Abundance is partly supply- and algorithm-driven rather than purely demand-driven. Conversely, when AI is **explicit** (Sora, Vibes), retention collapses.

### Cited Findings
- Kapwing (published November/December 2025): 21% (104 of the first 500) of YouTube Shorts recommended to a fresh account were AI slop, and about 33% were "brainrot." 278 of 15,000 top channels (the top 100 per country) were pure AI slop, with 63B+ views, 221M subscribers and about $117M a year in estimated revenue. India's "Bandar Apna Dost" had 2.4B views. Spain had about 20M and the US 14.5M subscribers to slop channels. — [The Guardian, 2025-12-27](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2025/dec/27/more-than-20-of-videos-shown-to-new-youtube-users-are-ai-slop-study-finds); [Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/blog/ai-slop-report-the-global-rise-of-low-quality-ai-videos/)
- Kapwing TikTok report (data as of May 2026, published 2026-06-09): 59% (294 of the first 500) of For You videos served to a new account were AI slop, as were 57.4% of 2,000 Kids-category videos (97% for #cartoonkids). The study manually analyzed 10,742 videos. The report also cites the BBC's Joe Tidy noting that likes on AI-backlash comments often exceed the original AI post. — [Kapwing, Jun 2026](https://www.kapwing.com/resources/the-tiktok-ai-slop-report/)
- Stanford Internet Observatory / Georgetown CSET (March 2024): 120 Facebook Pages posting 50+ AI images each drew "hundreds of millions of engagements." An AI-image post was among Facebook's 20 most-viewed posts in Q3 2023 (40M views). Comments suggest many users were unaware the images were synthetic. — [Stanford FSI](https://cyber.fsi.stanford.edu/news/ai-spam-accounts-build-followers); [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/from-shrimp-jesus-to-fake-self-portraits-ai-generated-images-have-become-the-latest-form-of-social-media-spam-226903)
- Detection ability is near chance. In Conjointly's September 2025 test (n=301 US adults; 6 real and 6 Gemini 2.5 Flash images), 42% felt confident but only 9% correctly identified at least 70% of images. Average accuracy was 49% for real images and 52% for AI images. — [Conjointly, Sep 2025](https://conjointly.com/blog/real-vs-ai-images-2025/)
- Getty image testing found AI imagery was more accepted when respondents thought it was real. — [CX Network/Getty](https://www.cxnetwork.com/artificial-intelligence/interviews/ai-generated-images-customer-trust-advertising)
- Explicit-AI products show weak revealed demand: Sora at about 1% Day-30 retention, and Vibes at about 2M DAU, with 40% of daily users arriving via in-app prompts. — [Medium/a16z data](https://medium.com/@cherc7888/sora-app-from-viral-sensation-to-1-30-day-retention-ef939c086136); [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/meta-vibes-ai-internal-documents-show-daily-active-users-2025-11)
- YouGov's own 2026 headline is that Americans "are worried about using AI – but that isn't stopping them from using it." Frequent users are far more positive. — [YouGov, 2026](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/55709-americans-are-worried-about-using-ai-but-that-isnt-stopping-them-from-using-it)
- A marketing blog claims AI content gets 38% above-average engagement (Tubular Labs Q4 2025) while 72% hold negative views (YouGov 2026). **Unverified**: I could not trace either number to a primary source, so treat it as illustrative only. — [Green Frog Labs](https://greenfroglabs.com/blog/ai-content-audience-says-hate-but-watches)

### Inferences
- The gap is partly a **knowledge gap** (people cannot detect AI), not purely hypocrisy. An app whose value is *guaranteed* authenticity sells certainty, which users say they want (76% Pew) and lack (53% not confident). That is a cleaner wedge than "less slop."
- The risk is that engagement-optimized feeds reward slop. An AI-free app may see lower raw time-spent than TikTok or Reels, and must monetize trust and community rather than pure attention.
- Kids' feeds have the heaviest slop (57% on TikTok Kids), which suggests a parent-oriented angle (see section 4).

### Gaps
- No rigorous public A/B data comparing engagement on labeled-AI versus human posts on Instagram or TikTok.
- No data on whether users reduced time or quit platforms because of AI slop. The CNET "reduce use" figure was not retrievable.

## 4. Which demographics care most? Niche or mass-market?

### Takeaway
Concern is broad, held by roughly half to three-quarters of adults, but **intensity** concentrates among artists and creatives (the Cara exodus), older adults (least comfortable with AI media), and Gen Z. Gen Z is paradoxical: the group most comfortable with AI media and the heaviest users, yet souring fastest. Anger among Gen Z rose 9 points to 31% in 2026. Parents are a plausible segment given kids' feed saturation, but direct survey data was not found. Strong "zero-AI" demand looks niche, at about 20%. The general wish for "real" is mass-market.

### Cited Findings
- **Gen Z (Gallup/Walton Family Foundation/GSV, fielded Feb 24-Mar 4, 2026, n=1,572 aged 14-29, MoE ±3.6):** 51% use generative AI weekly or more. Excitement fell 14 points to 22%, hopefulness fell 9 to 18%, and anger rose 9 to 31%; anxiety is steady at 42%. Among non-users, 60% feel anxious and 59% angry. 80% say AI will make it harder to learn. Gen Z is less optimistic than in 2025 that AI will enhance their creativity (-11 points). — [Gallup, Apr 2026](https://news.gallup.com/poll/708224/gen-adoption-steady-skepticism-climbs.aspx)
- **Young versus old (Reuters Institute 2026):** 22% of 18-24s are comfortable with fully AI-made news versus 7% of those 55+. — [Reuters Institute 2026](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/generative-ai-and-news-report-2026-standalone-tools-information-infrastructure)
- **Under-30s (Pew 2025):** 61% say AI will worsen creative thinking, versus 53% of all adults. — [Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/science/2025/09/17/how-americans-view-ai-and-its-impact-on-people-and-society/)
- **Gen Z shoppers:** 58% mistrust AI social content versus 53% of all shoppers. — [Mission Media/Retail Technology Show](https://missionmedia.asia/shoppers-mistrust-ai-generated-social-content-survey/)
- **Artists and creatives:** Cara went from 40K to 650K users in a week (June 2024) over Meta AI-training fears, later reaching more than 1M. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cara_(app)); [Cara blog](https://blog.cara.app/blog/finances-and-future-of-cara)
- **Parents and kids:** 57.4% of TikTok Kids-category videos sampled were AI slop (May 2026). — [Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/resources/the-tiktok-ai-slop-report/)
- **India and other regions:** India is both a major producer of slop (Bandar Apna Dost, 2.4B views) and a major consumer of AI feeds (704K Vibes DAU, Meta's largest Vibes market as of November 2025). — [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2025/dec/27/more-than-20-of-videos-shown-to-new-youtube-users-are-ai-slop-study-finds); [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/meta-vibes-ai-internal-documents-show-daily-active-users-2025-11)
- **UK:** among the least comfortable markets, with 5% comfortable with fully AI news versus 75% for human-made. — [What's New in Publishing / Reuters Institute 2026](https://whatsnewinpublishing.substack.com/p/ai-use-is-soaring-but-audiences-dont)
- Mintel's "US Marketing to Gen Z: 2026" names "continued erosion of trust" from "poor-quality or deceptive AI content" as the greatest threat, and authenticity as key. The data is behind a paywall. — [Mintel, Jul 2026](https://store.mintel.com/report/us-marketing-to-gen-z-market-report)

### Inferences
- The best beachhead segments are artists, photographers and illustrators (proven to migrate), plus UK/European users (lowest AI comfort). Parents are a hypothesis worth testing.
- India looks like a tougher market for an AI-free positioning: high AI-feed adoption and slop production. I found no India-specific attitude survey, though.
- Gen Z is the swing demographic. Their rising anger and analog interest favor the idea; their high AI usage and higher comfort cut against it.

### Gaps
- No survey data was found specifically on photographers' or parents' attitudes to AI on social media.
- No India-specific consumer attitude survey on AI social content was found.
- No sizing of how many users would actually *switch* to or *pay for* an AI-free app.

## 5. Digital authenticity, dumbphone, analog revival and deepfake trends

### Takeaway
There is a credible cultural undercurrent of "authenticity scarcity": Mosseri says it outright, Getty finds 98% tie authenticity to trust, and Gen Z shows analog interest. But hard quantitative data on dumbphone or analog adoption is thin, and marketers caution that interest in analog artifacts is not the same as an analog lifestyle. Inability to detect fakes (only 44% confident, roughly chance-level accuracy) is the most quantifiable driver.

### Cited Findings
- Mosseri: "authenticity is becoming infinitely reproducible." He advocates fingerprinting real media (Threads, about 2025-12-31). — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/01/06/adam-mosseri-instagram-warning-generative-ai-deepfakes/)

### Inferences
- The "authenticity" narrative is now mainstream and shared by platform leaders. That validates the problem, but also means incumbents will market provenance features such as C2PA-style signing and labels.
- An AI-free app's credibility hinges on enforceable verification (camera-capture provenance, detection, human review), since users themselves cannot tell AI from real.
