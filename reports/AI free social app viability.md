# Verified-human social can win a niche, not Instagram

An Instagram-like app that bans AI-generated content is **viable as a focused, capital-efficient community business, but not as a mass-market Instagram replacement, and not if "absolutely no AI" is read literally**. The demand is real and growing. **76% of US adults say it matters to know whether media was made by AI**, and concern is rising across every major tracker. But only about **21% of US social media users want AI content banned outright**. Most people want labels, and incumbents now offer those. Similar apps already exist. **Cara**, an anti-AI artist network with **1.5M+ users**, is the only one with real scale, and it runs on volunteers, takes no VC money, and was scraped three times in ten days in August 2026. Newer general-purpose "human-only" apps such as Rooverse, SocialHuman, Humanable and HumansOnly are tiny or unverified. The broader "authentic photo" category is mostly failures: Poparazzi shut down, Lapse removed its social features, and BeReal shrank before it was sold. A literal zero-AI promise cannot be enforced. Every phone photo passes through ML processing, detectors give **0–40% false positives** on real photos, and watermarks are easy to strip. The defensible version of the idea is therefore **"verified human capture"**: in-app attested capture and signed provenance, backed by a published rule for what counts as AI. Incumbents can copy a "see less AI" filter, and largely already have, but they are unlikely to impose a capture-only guarantee on billions of users. The founder should start with creators who have been harmed by AI (artists and photographers), monetize trust rather than attention, and treat the provenance technology as a second, B2B line of business.

## Demand is broad but shallow: three in four want to know, one in five wants a ban

Unease about AI content is now mainstream, and it is intensifying. In Pew's June 2025 survey of 5,023 US adults, **76% said it is extremely or very important to tell whether pictures, videos and text were made by AI**, and **53% lacked confidence they could do so**. The share more concerned than excited about AI rose from 37% in 2021 to **50%** ([Pew, Sep 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/science/2025/09/17/how-americans-view-ai-and-its-impact-on-people-and-society/)). Getty's 25-country study found almost **90% of consumers want to know if an image was AI-created**, and 78% said an AI image cannot be considered "authentic" ([Getty Images](https://newsroom.gettyimages.com/en/getty-images/nearly-90-of-consumers-want-transparency-on-ai-images-finds-getty-images-report); [CX Network](https://www.cxnetwork.com/artificial-intelligence/interviews/ai-generated-images-customer-trust-advertising)). Reuters Institute's 2026 report found **only 12% comfortable with fully AI-made news**, against 67% for human-made news. The UK gap is widest, at 5% versus 75% ([Reuters Institute 2026](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/generative-ai-and-news-report-2026-standalone-tools-information-infrastructure); [What's New in Publishing](https://whatsnewinpublishing.substack.com/p/ai-use-is-soaring-but-audiences-dont)).

The number that matters most for this startup is smaller. In CNET's survey of about 2,400 US social media users, **94% believe they encounter AI content while scrolling, 51% want it better labeled, but only 21% support a total ban** ([WGAL/Hearst](https://www.wgal.com/article/survey-shows-growing-frustration-ai-content-social-media/71311724)). The pollster and fielding dates of that survey are unconfirmed. That roughly one-in-five figure is the best available proxy for the core audience of a strict zero-AI app. Stated support, however, is not switching behavior, and no source sizes how many people would actually move to such an app or pay for it.

Revealed behavior complicates the picture in both directions. AI content draws huge engagement where people cannot spot it. Kapwing found that **21% of YouTube Shorts and 59% of TikTok For You videos** served to new accounts were AI slop, and that slop channels have earned **63B+ views and an estimated $117M a year** ([The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2025/dec/27/more-than-20-of-videos-shown-to-new-youtube-users-are-ai-slop-study-finds); [Kapwing TikTok report](https://www.kapwing.com/resources/the-tiktok-ai-slop-report/)). Yet people cannot tell real from fake. In a September 2025 test, **only 9% correctly classified at least 70% of images**, while confidence actually rose ([Conjointly](https://conjointly.com/blog/real-vs-ai-images-2025/)). So the gap between what people say and what they do is largely a knowledge gap, not simple hypocrisy. When AI is *explicit*, demand collapses. OpenAI shut the Sora social app in March 2026, about six months after launch ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/24/openai-shutters-short-form-video-app-sora-as-company-reels-in-costs.html)), after downloads fell 32% month over month ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/29/openais-sora-app-is-struggling-after-its-stellar-launch/)). Secondhand data puts its Day-30 retention near 1% ([Medium/a16z data](https://medium.com/@cherc7888/sora-app-from-viral-sensation-to-1-30-day-retention-ef939c086136)). Meta's Vibes reached about **2M DAU, under 0.1% of Meta's user base** ([Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/meta-vibes-ai-internal-documents-show-daily-active-users-2025-11)). This evidence shows people reject AI-*only* social media. It does not prove they will migrate to AI-*free* social media. What an AI-free app actually sells is **certainty**, which users say they want and know they lack.

The most intense concern sits with specific groups. **Artists** have already proven they will migrate: Cara jumped from 40K to 650K users in one week ([Wikipedia: Cara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cara_(app))). **Gen Z** is the swing demographic. Half use generative AI weekly, yet excitement fell 14 points to 22% and anger rose 9 points to **31%** in 2026 ([Gallup](https://news.gallup.com/poll/708224/gen-adoption-steady-skepticism-climbs.aspx)). **UK and European users** show the lowest comfort with AI content. **Parents** are a plausible but untested segment: **57.4% of TikTok Kids-category videos** sampled were AI slop ([Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/resources/the-tiktok-ai-slop-report/)), but no parent-specific attitude survey was found. India looks like a harder market. It was Meta's largest Vibes market, with 704K DAU ([Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/meta-vibes-ai-internal-documents-show-daily-active-users-2025-11)), and it is home to some of the biggest slop channels.

## Cara's 1.5 million users are the ceiling so far, and the category is full of failures

The answer to "is anyone already doing this?" is yes, but nobody is doing it at Instagram scale, and nobody with disclosed venture funding. The landscape falls into three groups: explicitly anti-AI platforms, "authenticity by format" photo apps, and proof-of-human infrastructure.

| App | What it is | AI policy and enforcement | Traction | Status (Nov 2026) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cara | Artist portfolio and social network | Bans generative-AI art; Hive detection plus human moderation | 40K to 650K in a week (Jun 2024); 1.5M+ (Aug 2026) | Alive, no VC, strained by scraping and legal costs |
| Rooverse (UK) | General "human-only walled garden" | AI detection at posting, user reports, small paid verification credit | Launched Apr 2026; about 6 App Store ratings | Tiny |
| SocialHuman (Helsinki) | Live in-app capture only, no uploads | Sensors, forensics, device attestation (self-reported) | Undisclosed | Unverified |
| Humanable / HumansOnly / Cosmos | Music, verified-human web network, curated images | Certification, human verification, curation | Undisclosed | Unknown or niche |
| Glass | Paid photography community | Ad-free, member-funded | Undisclosed | Alive, subscription |
| BeReal | Daily un-staged photo | Authenticity by format | 73.5M peak users; DAU 15M to about 6M | Sold to Voodoo for about €500M; far below peak |
| Locket / Retro | Close-friends photo sharing | Format-based | Locket 9M+ DAU, profitable; Retro about 1M users | Growing |
| Poparazzi / Lapse / Dispo / Vero | Novelty-format or anti-algorithm photo apps | Format- or values-based | Viral spikes | Dead, gutted or stalled |

Cara is the closest precedent and the most instructive one. It was founded by photographer Jingna Zhang, combines Hive automated detection with human moderation, and **deliberately avoided venture funding** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cara_(app)); [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/alternative-apps-to-instagram-ai-slop-for-artists)). By August 2026 it had **"over 1.5 million users"**. In the same month it was scraped three times in ten days: one scrape archived about 12M works, and another posted personal data to a torrent site. Zhang called Cara "a volunteer project with no financial means to respond," and launched a GoFundMe for legal and server costs ([Forbes, 2026-08-23](https://www.forbes.com/sites/robsalkowitz/2026/08/23/artists-built-a-site-to-escape-ai-scrapers-are-coming-for-it-anyway/)). Its MAU and revenue are not public, and some artists joined while keeping Instagram ([Creative Boom](https://www.creativeboom.com/insight/everyones-talking-about-cara-but-is-it-any-good/)). Three lessons follow. Demand spikes are **event-driven**, set off by an incumbent's AI policy change. The core buyer is a professional protecting their work, not a casual scroller avoiding slop. And an anti-AI platform **becomes a target**.

The 2025–2026 wave of general "human-only" apps shows the general-consumer version has not yet worked. Rooverse launched in April 2026 with AI detection at posting and a small paid "RooChip Lite" verification credit to deter bots, and had **about six App Store ratings** soon after ([Pressat](https://pressat.co.uk/releases/the-new-social-media-app-with-a-single-goal-a-human-only-space-with-zero-ai-content-9a4fdccea0d94fffd4abe4a67077a111/); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/rooverse/id6760191876)). SocialHuman requires live in-app capture, and its verification claims are self-reported ([socialhuman.dev](https://socialhuman.dev/no-ai-social-network)). Humanable announced an "AI-free" music platform for Q4 2025, and its launch could not be confirmed ([MusicRow](https://musicrow.com/2025/08/humanable-to-launch-worlds-first-ai-free-social-media-platform1/)). The market has no clear winner. That is white space, or a sign that investors see "no AI" as a feature rather than a company.

The "authentic photo" graveyard holds the more painful lessons. **Poparazzi** reached No. 1 on the App Store and raised $17M, then fell from about **4M MAU to 2,000–3,000**, shut down and returned capital ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/05/01/once-hot-photo-sharing-social-app-poparazzi-is-shutting-down/)). **Lapse** raised $30M. Its daily installs fell about 70% within weeks once forced invites were relaxed, and in December 2025 it removed its feed, profiles and chat ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/11/27/lapse-a-photo-app-that-forced-users-to-invite-friends-is-running-out-of-steam/); [Wikipedia: Lapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapse_(social_network))). **Dispo** lost its lead investor within weeks of going viral ([Wikipedia: Dispo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dispo)). **Vero** rode a 2018 artist exodus from Instagram to 3M downloads and then faded ([Phonandroid](https://www.phonandroid.com/vero-deja-3-millions-de-comptes-premiers-scandales.html)). That is the same trigger behind Cara's 2024 spike. **BeReal** went from 73.5M users to roughly 6M DAU before Voodoo bought it ([Wikipedia: BeReal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BeReal); [Voodoo](https://voodoo.io/news/voodoo-acquires-bereal)). Third-party estimates of its current MAU range from 16M to 40M. The survivors share one trait: they are **close-friends utilities**, not public discovery feeds. Locket reports 9M+ DAU and about $13.5M in revenue, and has been profitable since 2024 with a tiny team ([What A Startup](https://whatastartup.substack.com/p/he-built-an-app-for-his-girlfriend-and-ended-up-having-80-million-total-downloads)). Dealroom lists a much lower revenue figure. Retro raised about $21M from ex-Instagram founders ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/12/retro-a-photo-sharing-app-for-friends-lets-you-time-travel-through-your-camera-roll/)). Values alone ("no filters," "no algorithm") have never retained users. Daily utility has.

## "Absolutely no AI" is unenforceable; "verified capture" is enforceable

Taken literally, the founder's promise fails before a single upload. Every modern phone photo passes through ML-based computational photography such as HDR, night modes and denoising. OEM tools such as Magic Eraser, Galaxy AI and Apple Clean Up blur the line between editing and generating. Meta learned this in 2024. It stamped "Made with AI" on real photos, including a Pete Souza image, because photographers had used Generative Fill to remove objects, while "plenty of photos… clearly AI-generated" went unlabeled ([TechCrunch, 2024-06-21](https://techcrunch.com/2024/06/21/meta-tagging-real-photos-made-with-ai/)). A credible app needs a **published, tiered definition**. Under that policy, computational photography and standard exposure, color and crop edits are allowed. Generative object removal, AI upscaling, sky replacement and generative beauty filters are a disputed middle tier with a declared policy. Text-to-image, restyling, generative expand and face swaps are banned. In practice the promise becomes "no generated or substantially AI-altered media," enforced by policy plus provenance plus review.

Each enforcement tool has a hard limit. **Detectors** are unreliable and disagree with one another. In NewsGuard's May 2026 test, false positives on authentic photos ranged from **0% (Hive, Sightengine) to 40% (ScamAI)**. Flag rates on lightly edited images ran from 27% to 93%, and at least one tool disagreed on 35 of 45 images ([NewsGuard](https://www.newsguardtech.com/special-reports/leading-ai-image-detection-tools-mislead-online-users-often-declaring-authentic-content-fake/)). The sample was small, at 15 images per class. On real-world deepfakes, open-source detectors lose **45–50% of their AUC** compared with lab benchmarks ([arXiv 2503.02857](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.02857)), though a July 2026 paper reports the best commercial detector at about 0.90 AUC on images ([arXiv 2607.13234](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13234)). Cost is not the constraint: Sightengine charges about **$2 per 1,000 images** ([Paxmod](https://www.paxmod.com/blog/content-moderation-api-pricing-comparison)). False accusations are. By my estimate, a 1% false-positive rate at 1M uploads a day would wrongly flag about 10,000 human posts every day. False AI accusations against real artists are already a documented harm. r/Art banned an illustrator over a book cover that took 100 hours and called them a liar on appeal ([BuzzFeed News](https://www.buzzfeednews.com/article/chrisstokelwalker/art-subreddit-illustrator-ai-art-controversy)). On an app whose core users are human creators, a false ban is a brand-level failure.

**Watermarks and C2PA** only work as allow-list signals. Watermarks cover cooperating vendors only, and free tools strip C2PA metadata explicitly "to avoid Made with AI labels" ([MetaStrip](https://metastrip.app/blog/remove-c2pa-content-credentials-from-image)). Removal sites appeared within days of Sora 2's launch ([Neural Defend](https://www.neuraldefend.com/insights/topics/detecting-sora-videos)), and open-weight models carry no watermark at all. So a missing watermark proves nothing. Capture-time signing is also too thin to use as a mandatory gate. Pixel 10 and 11 sign every photo by default, with Pixel Camera rated C2PA Assurance Level 2 ([Google](https://blog.google/security/pixel-android-trusted-images-c2pa-content-credentials/)). Roughly 17 pro cameras can sign, often under a license ([Lumethic, vendor](https://www.lumethic.com/en/articles/cameras-with-c2pa-content-credentials)), and Nikon had to invalidate all of its certificates after a signing flaw. **No iPhone supports C2PA**. Apple's new Reference Image feature is opt-in, limited to the iPhone 18 Pro's main camera, readable only by Apple and switched off in the EU ([Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/09/apple-launches-a-new-way-to-prove-a-photo-was-shot-with-an-iphone-not-generated-by-ai/)). Requiring C2PA today would exclude most US users.

The strongest generally available option is **in-app camera capture with device attestation and per-capture signing**. It is BeReal-style capture hardened with App Attest, Play Integrity and hardware-backed keys, or bought as an SDK such as Truepic Lens ([Truepic](https://www.truepic.com/blog/truepics-new-sdk-will-power-trusted-photo-capture-across-the-internet)). It works on every phone. It has three known weaknesses. The "analog hole" lets someone photograph a monitor showing AI imagery. Rooted devices and virtual-camera injection can bypass attestation ([Guardsquare](https://www.guardsquare.com/blog/android-and-ios-app-attestation)). And it bars DSLR and edited uploads, which alienates the photographers and artists most likely to adopt. The workable design is therefore **tiered trust**. Attested in-app capture earns the top badge. Imports with valid C2PA from trusted devices earn a second tier. Other imports are screened by a detector, delayed, and published only after human review if flagged. Detector scores should only route posts to human reviewers, never trigger automatic bans. Creators get a protected appeal path where they can submit RAW files, layered files or process timelapses.

## Trust, not attention, pays the bills, and regulation helps detection without mandating a ban

No comparable platform has shown a large revenue engine. Glass sustains itself on **$6.99 a month or $39.99 a year** and says it is "100% funded by our members" ([Glass](https://glass.photo/help/what-is-glass); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/apple-store/id1528446339)). Bluesky, with 44M users, still had no public subscription as of August 2026 ([Social Samosa](https://socialsamosa.com/news-2/bluesky-explores-subscription-model-rival-x-8427264)). RevenueCat's 2026 benchmarks put median conversion at about **10.7% for hard paywalls and 2.1% for freemium apps** ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026)). A network-effects product needs freemium. My illustrative arithmetic: at 2.1% conversion and about $40 a year, 1M MAU yields roughly $0.8M a year. That covers a lean, photo-first operation, but not a venture-scale video network. Short-video users can consume 500MB–1GB of traffic a day, and CDN is the largest cost line ([Aliyun](https://developer.aliyun.com/article/1703787)). **Staying photo-first at launch is a cost decision as much as a product one.**

The most interesting upside is with advertisers. A July 2026 TAG/ANA/Fiducia study found that verification tools graded AI-slop inventory as "premium" **more than 70% of the time**. Advertisers paid a TrueCPM of $7.08 for slop versus $6.15 for clean inventory ([Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/media/ai-slop-is-fooling-advertisers-verification-tools/); [Fiducia](https://www.fiducia.eco/post/ai-slop-is-fooling-advertisers-verification-tools)). An audited "verified human" inventory has a plausible premium, but no source shows anyone paying that premium yet. The same study also counted human-edited, AI-assisted work as *not* slop, so advertisers do not share a strict zero-AI definition ([Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/analytics/ai-slop-programmatic-premium-grades-tag-ana-study/)). A B2B line, such as licensing the capture-verification pipeline or certifying human-made campaigns, is possible. It would compete with World's proof-of-human network, which a16z is promoting ([a16z](https://a16z.com/podcast/alex-blania-on-proof-of-human-and-building-worlds-identity-network/)), and with Meta's own stated direction.

Regulation is a net tailwind for detection, but it does not give a no-AI app any legal advantage. AI-labeling mandates are now live in **China (2025-09-01)** ([Loeb & Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2025/03/chinas-ai-labeling-measures-and-mandatory-national-standards-take-effect-september-1)), **India (2026-02-20)** ([Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/india-introduces-mandatory-labelling-for-ai-and-3hour-takedown-for-illegal-content)) and the **EU under AI Act Article 50 (2026-08-02)**, where a voluntary Code of Practice was finalized in June 2026 ([European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/code-practice-ai-generated-content); [Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/de/insights/2026/06/european-commission-publishes-final-code-of-practice-on-marking-and-labelling-aigenerated-content)). In **California**, provider duties started on 2026-08-02. Large platforms with 2M+ monthly users must surface provenance data from 2027-01-01, and **capture devices must support provenance from 2028-01-01** ([Orrick](https://infobytes.orrick.com/2025-10-17/california-delays-its-ai-transparency-act-and-passes-new-content-laws/)). These rules raise the share of AI content that carries machine-readable markers, which a no-AI app can auto-reject cheaply. California's 2028 device rule points toward exactly the positive "captured by a camera" signal this product needs. Their heaviest platform duties apply only at scale: India's start at 5M Indian users and California's at 2M monthly users. They normalize *labeled* AI, though, rather than banning it.

Other obligations apply from day one to any app that hosts user images. The **TAKE IT DOWN Act** requires 48-hour removal of non-consensual intimate imagery, including deepfakes, with penalties of $53,088 per violation ([WilmerHale](https://wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20260615-the-take-it-down-act-goes-live)). The **amended COPPA Rule** has required full compliance since 2026-04-22 ([Davis Polk](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/ftc-prioritizes-coppa-enforcement-new-compliance-obligations-take-effect)). The **UK Online Safety Act** demands "highly effective" age assurance, and Ofcom fined 4chan £450,000 ([Ofcom](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/protecting-children/age-assurance); [RPC](https://www.rpclegal.com/snapshots/consumer/summer-2026/ofcom-fines-4chan-for-breaches-of-online-safety-act/)). One further risk is my own inference, with no enforcement precedent found: a "100% human" promise is a factual marketing claim, so overstating it could invite consumer-protection scrutiny. That is another reason to promise "verified" rather than "absolute."

## Incumbents can copy a filter but not a guarantee

The biggest risk is that the problem gets commoditized. Users who just want "less AI" now have options elsewhere. **TikTok** added an AI-content slider in November 2025 and has tagged more than 1.3B videos as AI-generated ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/18/tiktok-now-lets-you-choose-how-much-ai-generated-content-you-want-to-see/)). **Pinterest** added "AI modified" labels and a "see fewer AI Pins" control ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/30/pinterest-launches-new-tools-to-fight-ai-slop/)). **YouTube** demonetizes "inauthentic" mass-produced AI content ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en-GB)). All three *reduce* AI content rather than eliminating it, and Instagram still has no master switch. The threat to watch is Instagram head Adam Mosseri's stated direction. He argues "it will be more practical to fingerprint real media than fake media" and has called on camera makers to sign images at capture ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/01/06/adam-mosseri-instagram-warning-generative-ai-deepfakes/); [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/ai-platforms-assistants/ai-slop-won-in-2025-fingerprinting-real-content-might-be-the-answer-in-2026)). If Meta ships a "verified real" badge or feed filter, the consumer pitch of a generic AI-free Instagram clone largely evaporates. The steelman for the startup is that Meta earns engagement from AI creators. It added an optional "AI creator" label in May 2026 that does not affect distribution ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/414862/instagram-unveils-new-ai-creator-account-label.html)). So Meta will badge real content, but it is unlikely to *exclude* AI content. A startup's moat is the exclusion rule plus the community it attracts, not the verification technology alone.

Four other risks are close behind. **Brand risk is asymmetric**: one viral AI fake on a "100% human" platform breaks the core promise in a way the same post on Instagram never would. **Becoming a target is expected.** Cara's scrapes and legal bills show that anti-AI positioning draws retaliation and data harvesting ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/robsalkowitz/2026/08/23/artists-built-a-site-to-escape-ai-scrapers-are-coming-for-it-anyway/)). Report-brigading ("this looks AI") is a likely abuse pattern, an inference not yet measured. **Retention is the historical killer**: every authenticity app above spiked and decayed, and no public D30 benchmarks exist for this category. **Funding is thin.** Some LPs had not backed consumer-dedicated funds in over a decade ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/20/in-a-good-sign-for-consumer-internet-startups-creator-ventures-raises-45m)), and consumer-social capital is going to AI-native social apps as well as authenticity plays ([Seedtable](https://seedtable.com/best-consumer-social-startups)). Finally, platform dependency cuts both ways. OS-level camera signing from Apple or Google would make verification cheap, and it would erode a startup's technical moat at the same time.

## Five moves turn a values pitch into a defensible product

First, **reposition from "no AI" to "verified human"**, and publish the rulebook. A public, tiered definition that allows computational photography, declares a policy on generative edits and bans generation outright protects the startup from Meta's 2024 mistake and from deceptive-claim risk. It also gives moderators a standard they can defend. The marketing line should promise something that can be proven, such as "every post here was captured by a real camera, and we show you how we know," rather than "zero AI forever."

Second, **build provenance-first capture as the core product, not an add-on**. Make attested in-app capture the default path and give it the top trust badge. Accept C2PA-signed imports from Pixel and pro cameras as a second tier, and hold unsigned imports in a delayed, detector-screened, human-reviewed queue. Plan to integrate Apple's verifier and California-compliant capture devices as they open up. Use detectors only to triage posts for human review, weight reporters by reputation, and give creators an evidence-based appeal path. This turns verification into a shield for creators against false accusations, which is a benefit Cara's users actively want.

Third, **launch into one atomic network that has already been harmed by AI**, Cara-style: illustrators, photographers, film and analog shooters, tattoo artists and art schools, seeded through invite scarcity and campus ambassadors (as BeReal did). Give them reasons to return daily beyond the values pitch: portfolios, commissions, print sales and creator discovery. Test UK and EU users and parents as second segments. Avoid India and general Gen Z discovery feeds at the start. Prepare infrastructure and moderation capacity for an event-driven surge. The next incumbent AI-policy scandal is the growth event, and Cara's cost strain came precisely from being unprepared for one.

Fourth, **monetize trust and stay lean**. Use freemium with a creator or "supporter" tier priced near Glass's level, and plan for low single-digit conversion. Stay photo-first until revenue justifies video CDN costs. Pilot an audited "verified human audience" ad product with brands that are already worried about slop. Treat the capture-verification pipeline as a possible B2B API for newsrooms, brands and marketplaces, with Truepic, World ID and C2PA vendors as likely partners. Locket's profitability with a small team is the right benchmark, not Instagram's.

Fifth, **plan the exit around the incumbents' direction rather than against it**. If Meta, Apple or Google adopt capture provenance, a startup with a trusted human-creator community and working verification technology becomes an acquisition target for a scaled app group (as Voodoo bought BeReal) or for an incumbent that wants provenance. Raising as an "AI-free Instagram" invites the question "why won't Meta just add a toggle?" Raising as "the trusted home of verified human creators, with provenance infrastructure underneath" answers it.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the founder's question. "No AI" is a negative claim that cannot be proven, sits on a definition no regulator or advertiser shares, and is the part incumbents are already eroding with filters. "Verified human" is a positive claim that can be proven. It is getting cheaper to prove as phones, cameras and California law move toward capture signing, and it is valuable precisely because fewer than one in ten people can tell real images from fake by eye. The opportunity is less a social network defined by what it bans than a trust layer with a community attached.

The evidence also suggests a realistic scale. The proven ceiling for an anti-AI visual network is about 1–2M users, reached on an outrage spike and sustained by people protecting their livelihoods. That makes a sustainable, profitable niche business plausible and a venture-scale Instagram challenger unlikely, unless the startup's verification layer becomes infrastructure other platforms use. The unknowns that most affect the decision are whether users will pay for or switch to guaranteed-human feeds (no study measures this), Cara's real retention, and whether Meta's "fingerprint real media" talk becomes a product. A founder can test the first two cheaply with a creator-focused beta before committing to the general-consumer vision.
